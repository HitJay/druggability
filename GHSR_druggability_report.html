GHSR is the receptor for ghrelin, the "hunger hormone." It regulates appetite, growth hormone secretion, gastric motility, and energy homeostasis. Constitutively active — signals even without ligand — which is rare among GPCRs and has implications for drug design.

Druggability Verdict

Bottom Line

GHSR scores 5/5 on small-molecule druggability — an Approved Drug target with 10 high-resolution structures and rich SAR. It belongs to the most successful drug target family (Class A GPCR) and has 2 approved drugs + 3 in Phase 3 clinical trials. However, both agonism and antagonism carry RED safety flags, demanding careful indication selection and therapeutic window definition.

Docking Recommendation

  • Antagonist / inverse agonist: Use 7F83 (only inactive-state structure, pocket is tighter). Consider ensemble docking with 8JSR to capture conformational flexibility.
  • Agonist: Use 8JSR (anamorelin, therapeutically validated) or 9UY3 (2.52 Å, best resolution) once released on RCSB.
  • Gi-biased agonist: Use 7NA7 or 7NA8 — distinct TM6 displacement from Gq-coupled structures.
  • Note: GHSR has high constitutive activity. Inverse agonists will have different pharmacology than neutral antagonists — factor this into your screening cascade.

Opportunity: First-in-Class GHSR Antagonist

GHSR antagonism is genetically validated (LoF-tolerant, favorable metabolic KO phenotype in mice: lean, insulin-sensitive, β-cell protected) and clinically unexplored. Indications include obesity, Prader-Willi syndrome, and substance use disorders. The key challenge is avoiding complete GH axis suppression — a partial antagonist or biased ligand strategy may provide the best therapeutic window.

Strategic Recommendations

#RecommendationRationale
1 Prioritize small-molecule modality SM tractability = 1.0; Approved Drug label; 10 structures; rich SAR. No need to consider antibody or PROTAC.
2 Pick indication before picking direction Inhibition → obesity, PWS, substance abuse. Activation → cachexia, GH deficiency. The direction determines the screening strategy.
3 Use 7F83 for antagonist docking Only inactive-state structure. If pursuing antagonism, dock against 7F83 first, then validate against 8JSR active state for selectivity.
4 Consider biased signaling GHSR couples to Gq/Gi/Go and β-arrestin. A Gi-biased antagonist could suppress appetite without full GH axis shutdown.
5 Peripheral restriction may improve safety CNS-penetrant antagonists risk anxiety (VTA GHSR KO phenotype). A peripherally restricted antagonist avoids this while retaining metabolic benefit.
6 Preclinical package: cardiac + endocrine + CNS Required: cardiovascular telemetry, GH/IGF-1/glucose monitoring, neurobehavioral battery, pediatric tox if chronic indication.
