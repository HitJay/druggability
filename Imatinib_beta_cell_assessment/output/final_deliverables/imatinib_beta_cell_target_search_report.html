Bottom-Line Conclusion: Imatinib acts in pancreatic beta cells through a 4-target Tier-1 intrinsic module — led by ABL1/ABL2 (ER-stress IRE1α rheostat) and DDR1 (the most abundant nanomolar kinase target in human beta cells) — while canonical RTK targets c-KIT and PDGFRα/β are virtually absent in adult human beta cells and act via islet stroma/vasculature.
  • 1. Primary Validated Functional Axis — ABL1 (c-Abl) & ABL2 (Arg) [Tier 1, FACT + OBS]: Imatinib binds ABL1 (Ki/Kd 1.0–219 nM, 54–99% occupancy at clinical free Cmax 260 nM; Vina -11.19 kcal/mol, 0.34 Å from crystal STI in 2HYY; 250 ps MD drift 0.66 Å) and its islet-abundant paralog ABL2 (Kd 6.0–12.9 nM, 95.3% occupancy; islet endocrine scRNA-seq 92.8 nCPM; Vina -11.84 kcal/mol, 0.19 Å in 3GVU; MD drift 0.73 Å). Under ER stress, cytosolic ABL1/ABL2 translocate to the ER membrane to scaffold and hyperactivate IRE1α (ERN1) endoribonuclease, driving terminal beta-cell apoptosis; imatinib or c-Abl siRNA knockdown blocks IRE1α RNase hyperactivity, prevents beta-cell death, and upregulates NKX2-2/GLUT2/insulin expression (PMID: 28380378, 17135364, 24835010). Direct imatinib binding to IRE1α itself is weak (Kd 10–30 µM, 0.9% clinical occupancy), proving that IRE1α attenuation is upstream ABL1/ABL2-mediated.
  • 2. Dominant Beta-Cell Receptor Tyrosine Kinase — DDR1 [Tier 1, FACT + OBS]: Across the entire human kinome, collagen receptor kinase DDR1 is the single highest-expressed nanomolar target of imatinib inside sorted human pancreatic beta cells (HPA scRNA-seq 81.40 nCPM in beta cells, 97.5 nTPM whole pancreas, endocrine IHC Medium — over 10-fold higher than ABL1, KIT, or PDGFR) and achieves the strongest docking score in our 25-pocket campaign (4BKJ, Vina -12.46 kcal/mol, 0.42 Å recovery, 250 ps MD drift 0.80 Å, anchored by GLU672 -60.5 kJ/mol, ARG789 -41.7 kJ/mol, and ASP784 -40.3 kJ/mol; measured Kd/IC50 0.7–42.7 nM, 85.9% clinical occupancy). DDR1 is an established islet endocrine progenitor/regeneration marker that senses islet basement-membrane collagen IV/fibrosis (PMID: 18938156, 21947380, 31207434).
  • 3. Non-Kinase Off-Target in Beta Cells — NQO2 (QR2) [Tier 1, FACT + OBS]: Quinone oxidoreductase NQO2 is the only potent non-kinase direct target of imatinib (Ki = 38.9 nM, 85.9% occupancy at clinical free Cmax) with substantial human beta-cell (13.77 nCPM) and islet endocrine (45.33 nCPM) expression. Reconstructing the biological homodimer interface around the catalytic FAD cofactor (3FW1, Vina -9.79 kcal/mol, 2.38 Å; MD drift 1.38 Å, anchored by GLN122B -42.0 kJ/mol and ASN161A -23.3 kJ/mol) confirms stable isoalloxazine π-stacking that blunts quinone redox-cycling ROS in beta cells.
  • 4. Why Named Targets c-KIT and PDGFRα/β Are Disfavoured as Intrinsic Adult Beta-Cell Targets [Tier 2, FACT + OBS]: Although KIT (1T46, -12.30 kcal/mol) and PDGFRA (6JOL, -11.62 kcal/mol) are potent Type-II imatinib binders, adult human beta-cell scRNA-seq shows 0.00 nCPM for KIT and 0.24 nCPM for PDGFRA (0.72 nCPM for PDGFRB; all IHC negative in adult endocrine islets; PDGFRA is epigenetically silenced during postnatal beta-cell maturation, PMID: 21993628). Crucially, genetic loss-of-function goes in the opposite direction of imatinib's protective phenotype: beta-cell-specific Pdgfra knockout (Pdgfrafl/fl Pdx1-Cre+, PMID: 41234234) and c-KitWv/+ mutation (PMID: 26253609) worsen beta-cell apoptosis and impair insulin secretion. Their in vivo contribution to diabetes reversal is non-cell-autonomous (islet stromal pericytes, vascular remodeling, and CSF1R/LCK-mediated macrophage/T-cell dampening; PMID: 19015530).
  • 5. High-Concentration In Vitro Off-Targets vs Falsified Hypotheses [Tier 3 & Tier 4]: At 3–10 µM concentrations used in isolated islet assays, imatinib partially engages beta-cell KCNJ11/Kir6.2 (KATP) (IC50 = 9.4 µM, 51.5% occupancy at 10 µM vs 2.7% at clinical free Cmax; MD-stable in the Kir6.2 ATP site 6C3O) and Src-family kinases FRK (Bsk/GTK) (Kd 0.32–1.5 µM) and LYN (Kd 0.20–0.89 µM). Conversely, the literature hypothesis that imatinib directly binds mitochondrial beta-oxidation enzyme ECHS1 (PMID: 34569605) is computationally falsified [Tier 4]: imatinib cannot fit the octanoyl-CoA tunnel (8ZRW, pose displaced 5.11 Å outside pocket; drifts 4.37 Å to 6.57 Å in 250 ps MD). Physiological INSR is cleanly spared (Kd 5–10 µM, pose displaced 4.51 Å outside pocket in 5HHW).
Methodological Scope & Evidence Discipline
Every claim is tagged [FACT] (curated ChEMBL/PubChem/HPA/literature), [OBS] (direct Vina docking or OpenMM MD observation), [MECH] (mechanistic synthesis), or [HYP] (unverified hypothesis). Docking scores and 250 ps restrained-protein MD interaction energies evaluate pocket geometry and pose stability, not absolute binding free energy (ΔG), and never override measured Kd/IC50 or clinical exposure math. Tier vocabulary is strictly capped at anchor (experimental imatinib co-crystal), computed-md, computed-dock, computed-negative, and untested.

2. Clinical Exposure & Occupancy Gate vs Beta-Cell Expression

A candidate protein can only mediate imatinib's beta-cell effects in patients if it satisfies both (i) meaningful target occupancy at clinical unbound plasma exposure (400 mg QD oral standard dose in CML and the Phase 2 T1D trial, Gitelman et al. Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2021, PMID: 34214479: total Cmax ≈ 5.2 µM, 95% bound to albumin/ORM1 → free Cmax ≈ 260 nM, free Ctrough ≈ 100 nM) and (ii) expression in pancreatic beta cells (HPA single-cell RNA-seq cluster c-10 sorted beta cells and c-3 islet endocrine cells). By contrast, in vitro islet survival and patch-clamp experiments routinely apply 1–10 µM imatinib in low-serum media, recruiting micromolar off-targets that are unengaged in vivo.

Structural Pharmacology Takeaways from the MD Layer
  • Conserved Type-II DFG-out Kinase Triad [OBS]: In all five high-affinity tyrosine kinases (DDR1, ABL1, ABL2, PDGFRA, KIT), imatinib remains locked within 0.13–0.68 Å of the crystal pocket center throughout 250 ps MD (drift 0.31–0.80 Å). Decomposition confirms that the amide-linked benzamide and protonated methylpiperazine form an intense electrostatic/H-bond clamp with the αC-helix conserved glutamate (GLU672 in DDR1, GLU286 in ABL1, GLU332 in ABL2, GLU644 in PDGFRA, GLU640 in KIT: -45 to -80 kJ/mol), the DFG-motif aspartate backbone/sidechain (ASP784, ASP381, ASP427, ASP810: -38 to -43 kJ/mol), and the gatekeeper threonine (THR701, THR315, THR361, THR674, THR670: -22 to -36 kJ/mol).
  • Dynamic Falsification of the ECHS1 Hypothesis [OBS]: Whereas all 9 true or micromolar binders stayed within 0.31–1.40 Å of their initial docked pose during MD, imatinib on mitochondrial ECHS1 (8ZRW) started 5.11 Å outside the octanoyl-CoA catalytic tunnel and drifted an additional 4.37 Å away during MD (ending 6.57 Å from the pocket center, computed-negative), ruling out direct competitive binding to ECHS1.

6. Actionable Wet-Lab Validation Roadmap for Beta-Cell Follow-Up

  1. Dissecting DDR1 vs ABL1/ABL2 in Human Islets / EndoC-βH1 (Primary Recommendation): Because imatinib inhibits both ABL1/ABL2 and DDR1 at >50–95% occupancy at clinical doses—and DDR1 is 14-fold more abundant than ABL1 in human beta cells (81.4 vs 5.8 nCPM)—compare imatinib head-to-head against (a) a selective allosteric ABL1/ABL2 myristoyl-pocket inhibitor (e.g. asciminib / GNF-5, which does not inhibit DDR1) and (b) a selective DDR1 inhibitor (e.g. 7rh / DDR1-IN-1) or DDR1 siRNA knockdown in cytokine/palmitate-stressed EndoC-βH1 cells or primary islets, reading out spliced XBP1 / IRE1α RNase activity, caspase-3/7 apoptosis, and GSIS.
  2. Testing NQO2 Contribution to Beta-Cell ROS Protection: Compare imatinib against the selective NQO2 inhibitor S29434 (or test menadione-induced ROS generation with and without NQO2 knockdown) at clinically relevant concentrations (250–500 nM) to quantify how much of imatinib's antioxidant effect in beta cells (PMID: 39085384) flows through NQO2 vs c-Abl.
  3. Avoiding High-Dose (10 µM) Artifacts in Secretagogue Assays: Keep in vitro beta-cell mechanistic assays at 0.25–1.0 µM imatinib when modelling clinical pharmacology; concentrations ≥5–10 µM directly block Kir6.2/KATP (KCNJ11) (IC50 = 9.4 µM) and depolarize the membrane independently of tyrosine kinase signaling.
